# hoel2018: Privacy and Data Protection in Learning Analytics Should Be Motivated by an Educational Maxim—Towards a Proposal

- **Reference:** Hoel, T., & Chen, W. (2018). Privacy and Data Protection in Learning Analytics Should Be Motivated by an Educational Maxim—Towards a Proposal. *Research and Practice in Technology Enhanced Learning*, 13, Article 20.
- **Full text:** [`pdfs/hoel2018.pdf`](../pdfs/hoel2018.pdf) (open access, from the journal website)
- **Topic:** Privacy in learning analytics
- **Thesis chapters:** 5 (Data protection), 6 (Reporting)

## What it is

A conceptual paper written while the authors were developing an international technical specification on privacy for learning analytics. It shows that legal justifications for privacy differ between countries, and that consent alone is not a sufficient basis in education, because processing is often justified by contract or legitimate interest. It ends with three principles for an "educational maxim": negotiate data sharing with each student; make the institution's use of data open and transparent; use the process to strengthen students' personal data literacy.

## What is relevant for my work

- **Lawful basis in my setting.** Trainees in professional courses often enroll through their employer. The paper's discussion of contract and legitimate interest as alternatives to consent fits this situation better than consent-based models (see gdpr2016, Art. 6).
- **Separating analysis from intervention.** The paper discusses an approach in which analysis is justified by legitimate interest and consent is sought only when acting on an individual learner. I apply the same split to visibility: staff and client companies see aggregated figures per cohort or company plus attendance and results per employee, which the course contract covers; detailed activity per trainee is visible only to the trainer of that cohort and to staff.
- **Transparency toward trainees (principle 2).** Trainees should be told, in the platform, what is collected and what their employer can see. This matches the GDPR Art. 14 obligation when data comes from the employer.

## Limitations

Conceptual, without an implementation. The translation of its principles into access rules is my own.

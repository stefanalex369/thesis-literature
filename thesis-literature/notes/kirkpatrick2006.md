# kirkpatrick2006: Evaluating Training Programs: The Four Levels

- **Reference:** Kirkpatrick, D. L., & Kirkpatrick, J. D. (2006). *Evaluating Training Programs: The Four Levels* (3rd ed.). Berrett-Koehler.
- **Full text:** book, not redistributable
- **Topic:** Evaluation of professional training
- **Thesis chapter:** 6 (Reporting and learning analytics)

## What it is

The standard model for evaluating training in organizations, with four levels: reaction (how participants rated the training), learning (what they learned), behavior (whether they apply it at work) and results (effect on the organization).

## What is relevant for my work

- **A framework from professional training, not from universities.** Most learning analytics literature (ferguson2012, macfadyen2010) comes from higher education. This model is what training organizations and their corporate clients actually use, so it connects my reports to the setting of the case study.
- **What my application can and cannot measure.** Level 1 comes from post-course feedback forms, and level 2 from exam results, which my application imports. Attendance and course activity are not one of the four levels; they are participation data that help explain level 2 results. Levels 3 and 4 happen at the client's workplace and are outside what the application can see. Stating this sets a clear limit on what the reports claim.
- **Why client companies want reports.** Employers pay for training and look for evidence of levels 2 and above. Attendance and exam results per employee are the part of that evidence the training provider can supply.

## Limitations

A practitioner model, criticized in the literature for implying a causal chain between levels. I use it to structure the reports, not to claim effects.

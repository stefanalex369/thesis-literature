# ferguson2012: Learning Analytics: Drivers, Developments and Challenges

- **Reference:** Ferguson, R. (2012). Learning Analytics: Drivers, Developments and Challenges. *International Journal of Technology Enhanced Learning*, 4(5/6), 304–317.
- **Full text:** publisher version paywalled (Inderscience), not included. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1504/IJTEL.2012.051816
- **Topic:** Learning analytics (foundations)
- **Thesis chapter:** 6 (Reporting and learning analytics)

## What it is

A widely cited overview of how learning analytics emerged as a field. It traces the drivers (large data sets from online learning environments, the growth of online learning, political and economic pressure to measure outcomes) and distinguishes learning analytics from educational data mining and from academic analytics.

## What is relevant for my work

- **Placing each report in the right category.** The trainer-facing views (activity and attendance of the learners in a course, compared with their results) are learning analytics in Ferguson's sense: they serve teaching. The views for staff and client companies (completion, attendance and pass rates per cohort or company) are closer to academic analytics: they serve the organization and its clients. My application is not educational data mining research.
- **Challenges.** The paper names the weak link between analytics and pedagogy and the ethical questions of collecting learner data. In my reports this means an activity metric is shown as an indicator for the trainer, never as a verdict on the learner.

## Limitations

Focuses on universities and schools. Professional training paid for by employers is less studied in this literature, which is why I add kirkpatrick2006 for that setting.

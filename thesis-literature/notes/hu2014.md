# hu2014: Guide to Attribute Based Access Control (ABAC) Definition and Considerations

- **Reference:** Hu, V. C., Ferraiolo, D., Kuhn, R., Schnitzer, A., Sandlin, K., Miller, R., & Scarfone, K. (2014). *Guide to Attribute Based Access Control (ABAC) Definition and Considerations* (NIST Special Publication 800-162). National Institute of Standards and Technology.
- **Full text:** [`pdfs/hu2014.pdf`](../pdfs/hu2014.pdf) (US government publication, free)
- **Topic:** Access control (attribute-based)
- **Thesis chapter:** 5 (Access control, security and data protection)

## What it is

The NIST definition of attribute-based access control (ABAC): access decisions are computed from attributes of the subject (the user), the object (the record), the requested operation and the environment, evaluated against policies.

## What is relevant for my work

- **Record-level scoping.** My rules "a trainer sees only the cohorts assigned to them" and "a client representative sees only employees of their company" are attribute rules: they compare an attribute of the user (assigned cohorts, company) with an attribute of the record (cohort, company). The guide gives the vocabulary and the model for them.
- **Combined with RBAC, not replacing it.** Roles decide which actions are allowed (sandhu1996); attributes decide which records. The guide discusses ABAC as complementary to RBAC, which is how I use it.
- **Where the policy is enforced.** The guide separates the policy decision from the point where it is enforced. In my application, the enforcement point is the data access layer that every endpoint goes through, which is also how I address A01 in owasp2025 and API1 in owaspapi2023.

## Limitations

Aimed at enterprise-wide ABAC with policy servers. I implement two simple attribute rules directly in code; the guide is the conceptual basis, not a blueprint.

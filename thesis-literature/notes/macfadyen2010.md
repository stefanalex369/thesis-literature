# macfadyen2010: Mining LMS Data to Develop an "Early Warning System" for Educators: A Proof of Concept

- **Reference:** Macfadyen, L. P., & Dawson, S. (2010). Mining LMS Data to Develop an "Early Warning System" for Educators: A Proof of Concept. *Computers & Education*, 54(2), 588–599.
- **Full text:** publisher version paywalled (Elsevier), not included. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2009.09.008
- **Topic:** LMS activity data and student outcomes
- **Thesis chapter:** 6 (Reporting and learning analytics)

## What it is

A proof of concept showing that tracking data from an LMS can predict final grades in online undergraduate courses and flag students at risk of failing. The authors correlated LMS activity variables with final grades and built regression and classification models from the significant ones.

## What is relevant for my work

- **Precedent for relating activity to results.** It shows that correlating LMS activity with assessment outcomes is an established, published approach. My application adds a source that a single LMS does not have: attendance at live sessions.
- **Active engagement mattered more than time online.** The strongest variables were related to participation (discussion and mail messages, completed assessments), not to time spent in the system.
- **The metrics do not transfer directly.** These were semester-long online courses where discussion forums carried much of the interaction. In short professional courses, interaction happens in live Zoom sessions and the final exam is on a separate platform. The corresponding signals in my setting are attendance at live sessions, completion of course materials and practice quizzes in Moodle, compared with the final exam result.

## Limitations

One institution, university courses, LMS technology from around 2008, cohorts much larger than mine. I can report descriptive relationships, not predictive models of comparable strength.

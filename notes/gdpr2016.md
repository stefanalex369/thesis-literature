# gdpr2016: General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679

- **Reference:** European Parliament and Council of the European Union (2016). Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation). *Official Journal of the European Union*, L 119, 4 May 2016, pp. 1–88.
- **Full text:** official text at https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj (not copied here; EUR-Lex is the authoritative version)
- **Topic:** Data protection law
- **Thesis chapters:** 5 (Security and data protection), 6 (Reporting)

## What it is

The EU regulation on the processing of personal data. It applies directly in Romania, so it applies to every system that stores trainee data.

## What is relevant for my work

- **Art. 5 (principles).** Purpose limitation and data minimization decide which fields the unified model imports: only what the management and reporting functions need, not whole CRM records.
- **Art. 6 (lawful basis) depends on who the contract is with.** When a trainee enrolls personally, processing needed to deliver the course rests on the contract with that trainee (Art. 6(1)(b)). When an employer enrolls its employees, the contract is between two companies and does not cover the employees in this way; processing then rests on legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)) or, for mandatory training, a legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c)). Analytics beyond what course delivery requires needs its own justification (see hoel2018). Determining the lawful basis is the organization's decision; my application has to support both cases.
- **Arts. 13 and 14 (information to data subjects).** In employer-paid courses the trainee's data often comes from the employer, not from the trainee. Art. 14 then requires informing the trainee, including about what the employer will see in reports.
- **Controller and processors (Arts. 4, 28, 30).** The training organization is the controller. External services that store trainee data on its behalf (the CRM, Zoom, an external LMS host) are processors under Art. 28. My application is operated by the organization itself, so it is not a processor but another processing activity of the controller, to be listed in its records of processing (Art. 30). Showing reports to client companies is a disclosure to another controller and needs its own basis.
- **Art. 25 (data protection by design and by default)** is the legal basis for design choices such as scoped access, minimal default views and pseudonymized exports.
- **Art. 32 (security of processing):** encryption in transit, access control and audit logging as concrete requirements.
- **Storage limitation (Art. 5(1)(e)).** Detailed data (Moodle activity, Zoom join and leave times) will be deleted or pseudonymized after a configurable period following the end of a course, by a scheduled job. Completion and exam records needed for certificates are kept as long as the organization is required to keep them.

## Limitations

I am not a lawyer. The thesis uses the regulation to derive technical requirements, not to give legal advice.
